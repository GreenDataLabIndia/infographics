A year of heat, cold and rain in ten Indian cities. One line for every day.

See the cities

Weather is more than numbers. It sets the rhythm of every day.

Each radial shows the climate of one city and the actual weather of every day in a single picture. You can see the pre-monsoon heat in Jaipur, the monsoon in Mumbai and the freezing nights in Leh at a glance. Hover over a chart to read any single day. Click it to open a larger view.

Temperature line. The closer a line sits to the centre, the colder the day's minimum. The further out it reaches, the hotter the maximum. The colour is the daily mean.

Rainfall. Rain is a blue circle placed on the day's temperature line. More rain means a bigger circle.

365 lines. One for each day of the year. January 1 is at the top (12 o'clock) and days continue clockwise.

Daily mean temperature, same scale for every city

The cities in detail

By default each radial is stretched to fill its circle so every city's pattern stands out. Colours always use one shared temperature scale. Switch to “Same scale for all” to compare the lengths of the lines between cities as well.

Why Climate Data Matters

Climate data is essential for understanding changing weather patterns, seasonal variations, and the growing risks associated with extreme heat and rainfall. By transforming daily temperature and rainfall data into interactive visualisations, this project makes complex climate information easier to understand and compare across Indian cities. It helps bridge the gap between raw data and public understanding, supporting climate awareness, evidence-based reporting, and informed discussions on climate resilience.

The project is designed to support the integration, organisation, and visualisation of city-level climate data. Its flexible data structure enables the addition of new cities and the updating of datasets, helping expand the scope of comparative climate analysis. By presenting temperature and rainfall patterns through interactive radial visualisations, the project makes complex climate information easier to explore, interpret, and communicate to researchers, journalists, and the wider public.
